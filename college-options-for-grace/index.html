College Options in Montana

Follow-up Research for Grace
October 2026

Grace,

Following our phone conversation, I looked further into what it would actually cost you to attend college in Montana, which degrees you could earn online from Helena, and how Chapter 35, Pell Grants, and scholarships could work together.

The most important finding is that you can earn an entire college degree online while continuing to live in Helena. You don't have to relocate to Missoula or Billings, and you don't necessarily have to stop working. Tuition isn't free, but available benefits could make college affordable without student loans.

Helena College

Accounting and Business Technology. Helena College offers online and HyFlex coursework in accounting and business. This is a practical route toward an associate degree, business-related employment, or transferring to a four-year university. Other career and technical programs are available locally, although some require in-person laboratories or practical training.

3. Starting at Helena College and Transferring

Complete the first two years at Helena College, selecting courses that transfer, then finish an online bachelor's degree at MSU Billings or another university. This could reduce total tuition considerably. Both schools should confirm in advance which credits will count toward the selected degree. You can also start directly at a four-year university online.

6. Scholarships Worth Investigating

Helena College Foundation Scholarships: Scholarships for associate and career programs, including technical and healthcare fields. Scholarship information.

Folds of Honor: Scholarships for eligible dependents of disabled veterans; higher-education awards can reach $5,000 annually depending on qualifying expenses and award. Scholarships.

Montana Gianforte Trade and Technology Scholarship: Up to $1,500 per semester for up to four semesters for eligible career and technical programs. Montana scholarships.

MSU Billings and the University of Montana also offer institutional scholarships. The goal is to cover as much tuition as possible through scholarships and grants before using Chapter 35 payments.

Pell and scholarships are additional education funding. You can attend part-time while working, though the Chapter 35 payment will be lower. There is no requirement to quit working.

8. Three Financially Reasonable Approaches

Option 1 — Helena College first. Lowest tuition; explore college, earn an associate degree or career credential, or select transferable courses for a bachelor's degree.

Option 2 — Direct online bachelor's degree. MSU Billings and the University of Montana offer established online degrees you can pursue from Helena while working. Tuition is higher, but Chapter 35 and potential Pell funding may make it manageable.

Option 3 — Start part-time. Begin with fewer classes, maintain more work hours, then increase enrollment if it fits. Chapter 35 payments decline with a lower course load.

None of these approaches requires relocating.

9. What We Still Need to Calculate

The biggest remaining question is the actual financial aid you would receive and how college fits alongside your job.

  1. Complete the 2026–27 FAFSA to determine Pell eligibility.
  2. Identify a few degrees that interest you.
  3. Request current tuition and fees for those specific programs.
  4. Confirm Chapter 35 approval with the school's VA certifying official.
  5. Apply for institutional and military-dependent scholarships.
  6. Compare funding with your expenses and desired work schedule.

Then we can determine whether there is a funding shortfall. If there is, your mom and I can discuss helping before you consider student loans.

10. Conclusion

The research produced a better result than I initially expected. You can remain in Helena and earn a degree through several Montana colleges, including complete online bachelor's programs. Tuition is not free, but it may be manageable with education assistance. A full-time student receiving Chapter 35 and the maximum Pell Grant could have about $21,984 in annual education funding before tuition and expenses.

The main decision is what you want to study and how many classes you want to take while working. There is no need to make a major change to your life to explore this opportunity. You already have the benefit. The next step is finding the education you want and arranging the finances so you can use it.
