# Jokelet Engine — production method
Version: 1.2 · Updated October 23, 2026
Status: working engine name; established recipe documented; repeatability validation pending.

## Purpose and boundary

Produce consistent, reusable Grill Sergeant jokelets through a controlled audio/video production recipe. A jokelet is a clean performance unit: joke delivery followed by its selected reaction and deliberately timed silence. It can stand alone with an outro or be assembled with other jokelets into an episode. Publication graphics, ambient sound and timed effects belong to the publication assembly unless a particular variant explicitly requires them.

This is a specialized engine for our terse dad-joke performance format. It is not a general film generator or a universal content-production method. Timeless joke material supports reuse; individual jokes still require checks for audience fit and intelligibility. One successful output demonstrates possibility. Repeated successful outputs, with costs and interventions recorded, demonstrate an operating method.

The user has authorized documenting the method and proving it across the next several episodes. This document does not authorize paid generation, scheduling or publication beyond the applicable production instruction. It does not certify pending assets or pretend future episodes have been completed.

Working name: **Jokelet Engine**. The resulting unit is the jokelet; Dad Joke Duty is the current show and Grill Sergeant the current performer. The name is provisional, not a final rebrand of existing tools.

## The product hierarchy

1. **Source master:** original uploaded or provider-generated bytes; retain original name, source ID and hash.
2. **Working derivative:** stabilized, cropped, trimmed or silent version, with its source relationship and processing recorded.
3. **Clean jokelet:** approved joke performance plus its approved reaction/hold; no burned publication thumbnail, recurring CTA, ambient bed or publication-specific graphics by default.
4. **Publication assembly:** one or more clean jokelets, approved outro/CTA, continuous ambient bed, selected graphics/captions and explicitly timed effects.
5. **Release:** the approved platform export and its posting metadata.

A standalone release can contain one jokelet and the approved outro. An episode can contain many. Neither requires modifying the clean jokelet master. Original sources and prior approved versions survive revisions.

## Established creative doctrine

- Comedy is performed. A text that reads correctly can still fail in voice, pause, emphasis or reaction.
- Keep joke wording terse, laconic and economical. Avoid unnecessary explanations, tag questions and chatty filler.
- After the punchline, Grill Sergeant says nothing in the standard format. Preserve the final spoken stop.
- The silent hold is part of the performance, not dead space awaiting removal. Restrained nonverbal behavior and ambient sound sustain it.
- The reaction supplies the second comic event: the listener/viewer feels the consequence of what GS just said. Perceived social jeopardy is a creative hypothesis to study, not a guaranteed audience response.
- Prefer restrained micro-movements compatible with the delivery and established character.
- A silent audio track does not establish a non-speaking performance. Review visible mouth motion.
- Categorization does not grant approval. Do not use an unapproved facepalm or another Review asset as an approved reaction.
- A spoken insert such as “Did you hear that?” is an explicit exception, not permission to add speech after every punchline.
- Preserve complete approved reaction durations by default. Shortening a reaction is an intentional, recorded edit.
- Tone exploration grades candidates independently by viability, comic function, voice, delivery and audience fit. Do not force a best-to-worst ranking or one winner. Several options may be viable.

## Baseline editorial sequence

**Setup and punchline delivery → selected reaction → sustained hold → next unit or approved outro.**

The joke performance can include its deliberate internal pause. Do not insert a listener shot into that pause by default.

Observed optional format: teller setup → listener anticipation → teller punchline → listener consequence. Anticipation welcomes or waits for the payoff; consequence responds after it. They are distinct editorial functions even when one source clip might supply both.

Record anticipatory versus post-punchline use as a future classification distinction. Existing pose metadata does not prove that an anticipation-role field, extra cut or corresponding automation is implemented. Keep that four-shot structure experimental until deliberately tested. It does not replace the baseline.

## Step 1 — choose an unproduced joke and prepare delivery

Record the canonical joke ID, selected spoken text, source/provenance, duplicate check and final spoken stop. Choose usable material that fits GS and the audience. Detailed premise/setup/hinge analysis belongs to optional study or resolving a concrete delivery problem, not a mandatory production examination.

Record delivery separately from spoken text: pace, intended setup/punchline pause, emphasis and final silence. Do not accidentally voice a direction or cue. Established voice preferences include a Firm baseline, Deliberate for slower delivery, Emphatic for written yelling, Forceful only for a short yelp and Quietly where suitable. Periods and capitalization can guide stress; these are preferences, not guaranteed synthesis controls.

Retain previous wording and the reason for any revision. Existing episode canon includes the approved bicycle wording “It was obviously two-tired.” A future callback is a planning item until its exact wording is approved.

## Step 2 — produce and approve the voice

For ordinary production, an existing approved recording may be reused when the task permits it. For the full-process proof requested October 23, exercise the ElevenLabs voice stage after startup access is ready. A reused voice take cannot count as proof of new voice generation. Keep authorization and actual usage/cost visible.

Keep original generation/download, exact input text, voice/model identity and settings, provider job ID, generation time, actual duration and audio hash. Record credits and actual charge when available, including failed attempts; distinguish currency conversion or estimates from provider-reported charges.

Listen to the entire take. Verify words, pronunciation, stress, timing, pause, intelligibility and final stop. Reject unwanted tags, extra speech, clipped endings and distracting artifacts. A text match alone is insufficient. Preserve rejected takes as rejected history, not approved production inputs.

## Step 3 — create the performance video

Use approved audio and the verified current GS identity/reference. Record the actual avatar/reference ID and source-image version; do not substitute an identity based on a similar name or an old assumption.

Record provider/render ID, prompt, composition/reference, audio hash and all available settings. Preserve the original render before correction. Portrait 9:16 is the production target. Use approved framing references and protect face, hands and graphic-safe areas.

Watch the full render for lip sync, identity, mouth behavior, motion artifacts, hands/props, pose and continuity. Record any repair rather than hiding it in an export. Existing HeyGen sources can supply performances or reactions, but each selected range needs its own suitability review.

## Step 4 — prepare the picture

Established order: **FrameLock/AnchorLock stabilization before MotionLock movement.** Keep a stable reference/optical anchor and avoid lateral jumps. Record reference, crop/scale, transformations and any frame corrections. Protect the face; do not improvise a zoom that cuts it off.

Master target: CFR 25 fps. Inspect actual source FPS, dimensions, rotation and timestamps first. A displayed editor counter may use a different frame convention from the source stream.

Record frame-rate conversion, trim boundaries, whether the end is exclusive, any padding and audio handling. A useful unambiguous range is startSeconds/endSecondsExclusive plus referenceFps. Never equate a screenshot's frame number with source-frame arithmetic without checking.

MotionLock and CapLock are named stages in the intended wider pipeline; their existence as planned stages does not certify completed automation. Preserve the current tested tools and log manual operations when needed.

## Step 5 — select and prepare the reaction

Use approved entries only for normal production. Review the full source range, including the lead-in and ending, before granting a new approval.

Metadata to retain:
- Stable asset ID, descriptive title, version and approval state.
- Source provider/Library ID or an explicit missing-source flag; original filename.
- Source and derivative hashes; parent asset; exact trim range and frame convention.
- Starting pose, ending pose, movement, expression, framing and scene.
- Visible mouth behavior and sound classification.
- Intended editorial use: post-punchline, anticipation, spoken exception or other, where recorded. This is an editorial requirement; support in current tooling must be verified.
- Associated joke/episode, reviewer, review date and reason for any rejection/edit flag.

For a normal silent reaction, remove unwanted audio from the derivative while preserving the original. Verify both no unwanted audio and appropriate visible mouth behavior. Keep different crops/durations as distinct variants. A byte-identical file can be recognized by its checksum; similar names or equal sizes alone do not prove duplication.

Check the entrance from the teller and exit to the next unit. Lighting, setting, eyeline, wardrobe, framing and props should make sense together. A categorized shot from an elevator or a source with burned captions may be unsuitable for the scene.

## Step 6 — assemble the clean jokelet

Bind the approved audio, performance video and selected reaction by stable IDs and versions. Record performance in/out and reaction in/out. Confirm that the performance covers the audio without cutting off speech or disturbing lip sync.

Keep the reaction's full approved duration unless an intentional trim is recorded. Specify whether its audio is retained. The default is silent; a spoken exception requires explicit selection.

Preview the entire jokelet, including its final hold. Check that no filler speech has been added, the cut has no flash/gap and the ending has not been clipped. Render a new version; never overwrite the original inputs. Keep publication thumbnails out of this master.

## Step 7 — assemble the publication

Choose the jokelet order. The existing episode target is five jokes: three core, one one-liner and one edgy selection, subject to actual approved material. Preserve planned progressively longer post-joke breaks where appropriate; exact durations are performance decisions, not an arbitrary universal number.

Add the approved saved CTA/outro as its own element. Add the ambient bed at this stage; a listener/reaction insert should not cause an unintended ambient reset or dropout. Keep speech intelligible and preserve the distinction between ambient sound and spoken words.

Place sound effects by deliberate event/time positions, with identity, level, in/out and any ducking/fades recorded. Do not assume that every reaction needs an effect or use a random effect to fill silence. Check that effects support rather than mask the performance.

Add publication-specific graphics and captions after picture/stabilization decisions. Check captions against the final voice, protect the face and avoid graphic conflicts. Create publication thumbnail/cover after the full assembly is known, retaining a clean jokelet master.

## Step 8 — release checks and recovery

Play the full export, not merely its first seconds. Check orientation, full duration, frame rate, sync, words, transitions, reaction holds, sound, captions, graphics and outro. Verify the actual downloaded file, not just a preview. Record codec/container and size; do not assume a browser render is the final target format.

Save the portable project JSON including local media, clean jokelet, publication video, source references, decision log and relevant processing logs. Keep earlier approved versions.

Factory/Publication builder browser storage is device-local. Save/share is an export request; confirm the file arrived in Drive. SxLock has separate project/export storage. A site snapshot does not preserve later browser-only edits. Restore a project into a separate working context when checking recovery; do not overwrite the only active project to perform a test.

GitHub holds the versioned tools, bundled assets and method records. Production Drive holds original/source archives and recovery copies. Link actual saved IDs/locations in the run record. A path in temporary scratch storage is not a durable production copy.

## Proving the engine over several episodes

Use the next **three completed episodes as an initial proof batch**. Three is a proposed initial batch, not a user-mandated episode count. Assign actual episode IDs when selected; do not invent missing episode numbers or treat the existing one-off flow test as a new completed episode.

Keep the baseline recipe fixed during the batch. Log any necessary repair, including manual intervention, and distinguish tool failures from creative choices. Do not conceal a failed run behind its successful replacement.

For each episode record:

- Exact edit/assembly decisions and actual output files/hashes.
- Start/finish and hands-on time when observed; attempts and failures.
- Paid generation cost and actual usage, or explicitly unknown/not applicable.
- Technical checks, full-playback approval and recovery verification.
- Every manual intervention, reason, stage and whether it repeated.

Evidence of repeatability means the documented procedure can reconstruct the correct products across the batch, keeps character/audio/editing behavior consistent, and exposes predictable work and costs. A final video that only works after undocumented repair is not a passed repeatability test.

A technically repeatable engine does not guarantee reach or comedy performance. Likes during silent passages are an observation that motivates study, not proof of a universal rule.

After the batch: identify recurring defects; revise the method/tool at the responsible stage; version the change; verify that change on a subsequent run. A setup-listener cut may be a later controlled editorial experiment, not an unrecorded mid-batch baseline change.

## Separate system — manuscript-led education videos

The education source of authority is the **manuscript**. The order is:
1. Identify the current manuscript/version and the specific chapter.
2. Break the chapter into its main parts, arguments, steps and examples.
3. Define what this video's viewer should understand or be able to do.
4. Craft a new spoken teaching script from those parts; it is an adaptation, not an automatic reading or arbitrary excerpt.
5. Develop shot plan, B-roll and graphics that explain the material.
6. Produce/review narration and performance; assemble and check teaching sequence.
7. Check content, graphics, captions, sound and final export against the source and approved script.

Maintain a trace from manuscript chapter/section to teaching-script version, factual/source check, scene, graphic and final video. Record omissions, changes and new examples explicitly. Where a subject depends on changing rules, facts or processes, verify it at production time rather than treating an old manuscript as permanently current.

Educational pacing follows explanation, comprehension and demonstration. Do not impose the jokelet's setup/payoff/reaction template or engineered comic silence on it. The same stabilization, captioning, file utilities and recovery practices may be reused without making the editorial engines identical.

The user does not consider this workflow ready for effective end-to-end automation. Keep manuscript interpretation, teaching adaptation, scene design and final approval under editorial control. Limited assistance with extraction, organization or rendering does not establish autonomous teaching judgment. “Maybe in a year” is a possibility to revisit, not a delivery promise or scheduled commitment.
